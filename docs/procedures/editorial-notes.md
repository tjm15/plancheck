# Authored explanations for the procedural review guide

These explanations are maintained by hand. The documentation builder combines
these notes with the current model, source catalogue and evaluated examples.
Edit these notes for interpretation/rationale; edit the model for an assertion,
predicate or fact definition; rebuild the readable pages afterwards.

## stage:legacy.preparation

The authority prepares its plan and engages the appropriate bodies and people.
The model represents the invitation and consideration duties in regulation 18.
It does not give this stage an automatic six-week minimum. The later publication
consultation has a different legal basis and a prescribed minimum. Evidence of
engagement and evidence that responses influenced consideration are different
inputs; an invitation register cannot establish both.

## stage:legacy.publication

Regulation 19 is publication of the proposed submission material; regulation 20
is the opportunity to make representations on it. The six-week minimum comes
from regulation 17's definition of the statement of representations procedure.
Here the evaluator can calculate the calendar interval. It relies on a separate
reviewed observation for the contents of the publication pack and notifications.

## stage:legacy.readiness

The pre-examination checklist supports preparation for submission. Its place in
the Inspectorate's procedure guide does not turn it into one of the new system's
statutory gateways. The checklist and ten-working-day response aim are represented
as guidance/service practice. The statutory readiness condition sits in the
submission stage under saved PCPA section 20(2).

## stage:legacy.submission

The authority submits the plan and the regulation 22 supporting documents to the
Secretary of State. Some items have conditions: the policies map depends on a
change to the adopted map, and representations depend on their receipt. The
model also records statutory readiness and publication/notification obligations.
Each document Boolean records supply; it does not assess its substance. The
2012 instrument is applied through the 2026 saving, using revised rather than
unqualified as-made text.

## stage:legacy.examination

The Inspector examines the plan and considers representations. For the statutory
hearing entitlement, the saved provision requires a representation seeking change
and a request to be heard. The authority's six-week hearing notice and notification
to non-withdrawn regulation 20 representors are separate duties. Participation
outside this entitlement can still be allowed; a false entitlement condition does
not mean that a person is prohibited from attending.

## stage:legacy.modifications

If the authority asks, the saved legislation provides for recommendations of
modifications that make the plan sound and meet the saved legal requirements.
The authority-request condition is important when comparing the new system.
Consultation on main modifications has a six-week minimum in Inspectorate guidance;
that normative status is retained even though environmental and fairness duties
may have their own legal bases beyond this package.

## stage:legacy.pause

The legacy guide permits further work during a pause and describes a usual
six-month overall limit. This model records that as qualified guidance. It does
not import the new legislation's statutory pause mechanism or its prescribed
maximum into the saved regime. The evaluator takes the duration assessment as an
input; it does not determine whether a particular departure from guidance is justified.

## stage:legacy.reporting

At the end of examination the Inspector makes reasoned recommendations, and the
authority publishes them. Under the saved statutory text, a recommendation of
non-adoption can coexist with recommended curative main modifications. The saved
adoption provision then permits adoption with those modifications. The stage label
also mentions QA and fact checking, but their administrative steps are not encoded
as individual checks in this release.

## stage:legacy.adoption

The authority's resolution and adopted content must fall within saved PCPA
section 23. Adoption as recommended and adoption with required main modifications
are distinct statutory routes, with limits on additional modifications. Regulation
26 then governs publication and notices. The Inspector's recommendation and the
authority's adoption resolution are separate acts; one does not prove the other.

## stage:legacy.withdrawal

This stage records what must be published and notified following withdrawal,
and the specified cessation of document availability under regulation 27. It
does not model every circumstance in which withdrawal can occur or determine
that withdrawal is the correct substantive decision.

## stage:new.timetable

The authority publishes and maintains a timetable and a notice of intention to
prepare the plan. The timetable check is currently a broad reviewer-supplied
conclusion covering selected regulations 4, 6 and 8, rather than separate automated
checks of each milestone and revision. The five-year replacement-plan rule concerns
an existing new-system plan after adoption/approval. Initial entry into the new
system has separate rollout provisions that are not executable here.

## stage:new.scoping

Scoping invites representations on what the plan ought to contain. The scoping
notice cannot precede the notice of intention. There is no fixed six-week minimum
assigned to scoping in this model. The subsequent content/evidence and proposed-plan
consultations have their own minimum periods and different document packs.

## stage:new.gateway1

Gateway 1 is the authority's self-assessment of readiness. It covers programme,
governance, engagement, anticipated plan content and relevant SEA obligations.
The authority publishes it only after both the statutory four-month notice period
and the scoping deadline permit. The evaluator calculates the day-after-scoping
comparison but asks a reviewer to resolve the calendar-month condition. Publication
of the scoping summary is a further, later act.

## stage:new.content

The authority consults on proposed content and evidence after publishing its
scoping summary. The consultation lasts at least six weeks from notice publication.
Its summary must follow the deadline and account for representations, or their
absence. This is preparatory content/evidence consultation; the later consultation
on the proposed plan has an eight-week minimum.

## stage:new.gateway2

An appointed assessor provides observations and advice during preparation. The
authority must request that advice after the content/evidence summary and send
what it considers necessary on the request date. The legislation does not specify
a fixed draft-plan/map pack at this gateway. The guide recommends useful supporting
material, and those recommendations remain advisory. Separate rules cover the
indicative assessment duration and publication of advice and supplied material.

## stage:new.publication

After gateway 2 advice is published, the authority consults on its proposed plan
and prescribed supporting material for at least eight weeks. It must have regard
to gateway advice and consultation responses; that is not automatically obedience
to every recommendation. An operative spatial development strategy and qualifying
supplementary plans introduce additional conditional steps. The consultation
summary must follow the representation deadline.

## stage:new.gateway3

Gateway 3 is the appointed assessor's readiness assessment against the prescribed
regulation 32 requirements. The authority supplies the regulation 17 documents,
including completed compliance and soundness statements. This stage has three
separate questions: was the required material supplied at the right time; what
did the assessor conclude about each requirement; and were advice and successful
completion material published correctly? The model records the assessor's findings
as inputs. It does not infer soundness from the presence of documents.

## stage:new.gateway3_repeat

A failed gateway 3 leads to work addressing the stated reasons and a further
assessment with updated documents. The process can repeat. The current executable
rule compresses the remedy/request/update/iteration duties into one reviewed
Boolean. The graph shows a loop, but the evaluator does not store or replay each
assessment cycle. This is a specific implementation limitation visible to review.

## stage:new.submission

Successful prescribed-requirements advice triggers submission under PCPA section
15D(1). The examination submission pack under regulation 34 differs from the
gateway 3 pack, even where document names overlap. The two sets of supply facts
must not be treated as interchangeable. The package records publication of the
submission date and material, with detailed mechanics still requiring review.

## stage:new.examination

The examiner considers whether it is reasonable to conclude that the plan is
sound. Any representor requesting to be heard has the new statutory entitlement;
the saved system's additional seeking-change condition is absent. Six-week hearing
notice is a separate procedural check. The guide's six-month examination expectation
is a target, distinct from the statutory maximum for an individual pause.

## stage:new.modifications

On completing examination, if modifications would make the plan sound, the new
statutory recommendation includes those modifications, adoption with them, and
reasons. It does not depend on the saved system's LPA-request condition. Consultation
and necessary environmental/habitats assessment work are recorded as guidance in
this package; their full underlying substantive legal framework needs a separate model.

## stage:new.pause

The examiner may pause for further work, notifying the authority and Secretary
of State of the matters and period. The period cannot begin before notification
and cannot exceed the prescribed six months. The authority responds before expiry
and publishes its response. At the end, withdrawal requires the examiner's conclusion
that unresolved matters leave no prospect of a sound plan; otherwise examination
resumes. Months and actual before-expiry delivery are reviewed inputs, not fixed-day
approximations.

## stage:new.reporting

A completed examination can lead to adoption, adoption with modifications or
withdrawal recommendations, each with the relevant statutory conditions and reasons.
The withdrawal branch excludes an examination that is paused or to be paused.
Publication is a separate authority obligation. As in the legacy reporting stage,
QA/fact-check procedure appears in the stage description but is not exhaustively
encoded as separate executable duties.

## stage:new.adoption

The authority adopts by resolution in accordance with PCPA section 15EA and the
Inspector's recommendation, including required modifications and the statutory
limit on additional changes. Publication of the adopted plan and adoption statement
then follows regulation 39. Compliance of adopted content is supplied as an
attributable judgement, rather than inferred from a resolution's existence.

## stage:new.withdrawal

Regulation 38 requires withdrawal publicity, notifications and specified changes
to document availability while retaining the withdrawal statement and timetable.
This stage describes those consequences. It is not a complete account of all
withdrawal powers, directions or routes into withdrawal.

## rule:new.gateway2_necessary_material

Read regulation 26(3): it fixes the **authority's judgement of necessity** and
**same-day transmission**, not a named mandatory draft-plan/map pair. The predicate
therefore combines one reviewed necessity/supply observation with equality of two
calendar dates. A false necessity observation can fail the assertion; an absent
recommended draft plan cannot substitute for that observation. Review what the
authority actually considered necessary and what was sent.

## rule:new.gateway2_draft_plan

The advisory-gap output answers whether the recommended emerging detail was supplied.
It does not answer whether the authority complied with regulation 26(3). To audit
the legal duty, inspect the separate necessary-material rule and evidence. The guide
allows useful policy detail without requiring a full emerging draft plan.

## rule:new.gateway3_soundness

This is a **statement of soundness**, supplied by the authority, in the prescribed
pack. The single Boolean establishes only its provision. It neither assesses the
strength of the statement nor records the examiner's eventual conclusion. Review
the definition in regulation 17 and the sending duty in regulation 31(3) separately.

## rule:new.gateway3_test_j

This records the appointed assessor's conclusion that the regulation 34 documents
specified in regulation 32(j) have been prepared. It is not recomputed from the
pack-presence facts. That separation preserves attribution but also means inconsistent
inputs can currently coexist: the evaluator has no cross-fact contradiction engine.

## rule:new.gateway3_repeat

One Boolean currently stands in for a multi-step legal process. It is reviewable
as a recorded conclusion, but unsuitable for automatically tracking a real remedial
programme until each iteration, reason, action and resubmission is represented.

## rule:new.pause_response

The response-date and expiry-date facts alone cannot decide a same-day delivery
question. The predicate requires an expressly reviewed before-expiry finding,
plus reviewed contents and publication. The source's response deadline and the
subsequent publication timing are different requirements; the bundled observations
must preserve that distinction in their evidence.

## rule:new.timetable

The current predicate asks for the reviewer's overall timetable conclusion. It
does not implement the individual monthly updates or same-day milestone revisions.
The broad claim in the rule therefore exceeds what can be independently calculated
by the evaluator. Treat its result as an attributed review record.

## rule:legacy.publication_pack

This rule compresses document availability, notice contents and recipient duties
into a single observation. Its input definition says what is bundled, but it cannot
show which sub-obligation failed. Decomposing the bundle is a future refinement,
not something the present passing Boolean proves has been implemented.

## rule:legacy.mm_request

The request is a condition of the saved modification-recommendation provision.
If the authority did not ask, this selected duty is not applicable; that is different
from concluding that the plan is sound, can be adopted unchanged, or cannot be
modified through any other lawful route.

## rule:new.begin_replacement

The five-year period is a different question from the initial rollout date for an
authority entering the 2026 system. It is currently resolved through a reviewer
observation; the evaluator does not calculate calendar years or identify the relevant
adoption/approval event from documents.

## case:legacy-six-weeks

The legacy publication date and deadline are 42 calendar days apart. The evaluator
can calculate that interval and satisfy the selected legacy minimum. The new-system
proposed-plan rule is not applicable because the declared and evidenced regime is
legacy. No conclusion follows about the adequacy of the consultation documents.

## case:new-plan-six-weeks

The same 42-day interval is insufficient for the new proposed-plan consultation's
56-day minimum. The result is a failed selected statutory assertion. The fixture
does not establish whether the consultation is already complete, whether the
deadline will be extended or any consequence for the whole plan.

## case:gateway2-no-draft-plan

The authority has supplied the material it judged necessary, on the request date,
but no emerging draft plan or map. The separate advisory recommendations therefore
show gaps while the regulation 26(3) assertion is satisfied on the supplied judgement
and dates. This example is deliberately capable of distinguishing legal necessity
from useful supporting practice.

## case:gateway3-missing-soundness

At gateway 3, the authority expressly records that the statement of soundness was
not supplied. The selected prescribed-document assertion is therefore false. This
is not the same question as whether the plan itself is sound. If the inventory were
unknown instead of explicitly negative, the evaluator would return unknown.

## case:legacy-cutoff-inclusive

With an evidenced saved legal basis, submission on 31 December 2026 satisfies the
ordinary inclusive deadline used by the routing code. The plan is routed to legacy.
The collection page's date alone cannot override the statutory saving.

## case:late-legacy-unverified-direction

A later date has been entered, but the existence/applicability of the direction is
not verified. The routing code cannot establish saving eligibility and does not
convert the plan into the new system. A date field is not evidence of a lawful direction.

## case:gateway3-not-soundness-verdict

Successful gateway advice and submission can support the submission assertion.
The completed-examination adoption recommendation has separate conditions, so a
gateway pass does not make it applicable. Document provision, assessor readiness
advice and examiner soundness judgement remain distinct facts.

## case:new-pause-limit

The supplied reviewed pause-length observation is false, while the separate
examination-target observation is also false. The former fails a statutory assertion;
the latter misses a policy expectation. The example does not itself calculate six
calendar months from dates or independently verify either duration observation.

## case:pause-response-date-only

The response and expiry have the same calendar date. Neither the actual expiry
instant nor delivery-before-expiry judgement is supplied. The answer remains unknown,
even though the response contents and publication are recorded as complete.

## case:legacy-non-adoption-with-curative-modifications

The Inspector's legacy non-adoption recommendation coexists with curative main
modifications requested by the authority. The separate adopted-content observation
records that saved section 23 permits adoption with those modifications. This is a
set of attributed findings; the evaluator does not inspect the modified plan to
produce that adoption judgement.
